What Happens When a Football Match Ends in a Draw?

A football match does not always produce a winner. Sometimes both teams finish with the same number of goals, leaving supporters wondering what happens next. Discover how draws work in league matches, knockout competitions and tournament football.

Match Rules Football Guide Easy Explanation

Introduction

Football is a competitive sport, but not every match ends with one team celebrating a victory. A game can finish with both sides scoring the same number of goals, or with neither team finding the net. When this happens, the official result is known as a draw.

A draw is a normal part of football. It can happen in domestic leagues, international fixtures, cup competitions and friendly matches. For example, a match finishing 1–1 means each team has scored once. A 0–0 result means neither side has scored. In both situations, the teams have an equal score when the match ends.

However, what happens after a draw depends on the competition's format. In many league matches, both teams simply receive points and move on to their next fixture. In certain knockout competitions, the organisers must determine which team advances. That can involve extra time or a penalty shootout.

Understanding these differences makes it easier to follow football results and competition standings. Let's look at the rules and the situations that can follow a level score.

What Does a Draw Mean in Football?

A draw occurs when two teams have scored an equal number of valid goals by the end of the match. According to Law 10 of the Laws of the Game, the team with more goals wins. If both teams score the same number, or neither team scores, the match is drawn.

This rule applies to different scorelines. A 0–0 match is just as much a draw as a 2–2 or 3–3 match. The number of goals does not change the basic meaning of the result.

0–0 Goalless draw
1–1 One goal each
2–2 Two goals each

A draw does not mean that the match was abandoned or that the referee could not decide the result. It is an official outcome when the teams finish level under the applicable competition rules.

Remember: A drawn match is a completed result. It does not automatically mean that the teams must play extra time or take penalties.

What Happens in a League Match?

In most domestic football leagues, a draw means that both teams receive one point. The teams do not normally continue playing after the regular match has ended. Instead, the result is recorded in the league table, and each club prepares for its next scheduled fixture.

League competitions usually award three points for a win, one point for a draw and zero points for a defeat. This system rewards winning teams while still recognising that a side has earned a result by sharing the points.

How are league points calculated?

Match Result Points Awarded
Win 3 points
Draw 1 point per team
Loss 0 points

For example, imagine that Team A and Team B finish their match at 1–1. Both teams receive one point. Neither side gets the three points reserved for a victory, but neither loses points through a defeat in the standard league points system.

Can a draw affect the league table?

Yes. League positions are often determined by total points, so even one additional point can matter over a long season. A team fighting to avoid relegation may value a draw against a strong opponent. Similarly, a club competing for the title may feel disappointed if it drops points after leading during the match.

If two or more teams finish with the same points, the competition's tie-breaking rules determine their positions. These rules can include goal difference, goals scored, head-to-head records or other criteria specified by the league.

What Happens When a Knockout Match Ends in a Draw?

Knockout competitions work differently from ordinary league fixtures. In a knockout match, one team may need to progress to the next round while the other is eliminated. A simple draw may therefore be insufficient to decide the competition outcome.

The tournament regulations explain how a winner is selected. Depending on the competition format, the teams may play extra time, take penalties or use another permitted procedure. Supporters following European competitions can learn more about tournament structures in this Champions League league-phase guide.

1. Extra time

Some knockout matches include extra time when the score remains level after normal playing time. Under the Laws of the Game, extra time consists of two equal periods, each no longer than 15 minutes. The exact arrangements are determined by the competition regulations.

Extra time gives both teams another opportunity to score during open play. Players may have to manage tiredness, concentration and tactical changes while trying to avoid conceding a goal.

If one team scores more goals during extra time, it wins the match. If the aggregate score remains level at the end of the required periods, the competition may use a penalty shootout.

2. Penalty shootouts

A penalty shootout is used in competitions that require a winner when the relevant match or tie remains level. It takes place after the match has ended and follows a specific procedure.

In the standard format, both teams initially take five kicks each, alternating between sides. Different players take the kicks, and the team with more successful attempts after the initial series wins.

If the score remains level after both teams have taken five kicks, the shootout continues in sudden-death rounds. Each side takes another kick, and the contest ends when one team scores while the other fails from the same number of attempts.

Important distinction: A penalty shootout is not the same as awarding a penalty kick during normal play. It is a separate procedure used to determine the winner after the match has ended.

How Does a Penalty Shootout Decide the Winner?

Penalty shootouts are often among the most dramatic moments in football. They place pressure on both kickers and goalkeepers because each attempt can influence the final result. However, the procedure is structured rather than random.

The referee follows the competition rules and the relevant Laws of the Game. The teams alternate their kicks, and each eligible player must take a kick before a player can take a second one in the same sequence.

What if both teams score the same number?

If both teams remain level after their first five kicks, the shootout continues. This is commonly called sudden death. The teams keep taking one kick each until one side has scored more goals than the other after the same number of attempts.

For example, if both teams score four times from their first five attempts, they continue. If Team A scores in the next round and Team B misses, Team A wins. There is no need to complete additional rounds once the winner has been determined.

Can every player take a penalty?

Only eligible players can participate in the shootout. The Laws of the Game set out who may take kicks, including rules about players who were sent off during the match. The referee also ensures that the teams have equal numbers of eligible players where the rules require it.

What Happens in a Two-Legged Football Tie?

Some competitions use two matches to decide which team advances. These are known as two-legged ties. Each team normally plays one match at home and one away, although the order depends on the tournament schedule.

The combined goals scored across both matches form the aggregate score. This means a team can lose the first leg but still qualify by scoring enough goals in the second leg.

Example: Aggregate Score

First leg: Team A 1–0 Team B

Second leg: Team B 2–1 Team A

Aggregate: Team A 2–2 Team B

The overall score is level. The competition regulations determine whether extra time, penalties or another permitted procedure is used to decide which team advances.

It is important not to assume that an away goal automatically gives a team the advantage. The away-goals rule has been removed from many major competitions. Always check the specific tournament regulations before deciding how a tied aggregate score will be resolved.

How Can a Draw Affect a Team's Season?

A draw may look like a simple result, but its importance can change depending on the stage of a season. Teams have different objectives, and the value of one point is not always the same for every club.

For title challengers

A team competing for the league title usually wants to collect as many points as possible. A draw can feel like a missed opportunity, particularly when the team was expected to win. If a direct title rival wins its fixture on the same day, the gap between the clubs may increase.

For teams fighting relegation

A point can be valuable for a team near the bottom of the table. Securing a draw against a higher-ranked opponent may help a club move closer to safety. Consistency and avoiding defeats can become important during a difficult part of the season.

For teams competing for qualification

Clubs aiming to qualify for continental competitions may also pay close attention to draws. A point can keep a team in contention, but repeated draws may allow competitors to build a points advantage.

The result should therefore be judged in context. A draw away from home against a strong opponent might be considered positive, while a home draw against a struggling side may disappoint supporters.

Why Are Draws Common in Football?

Football is a low-scoring sport compared with many other team games. A single goal can have a major influence on the final score, and both teams may spend long periods defending effectively. As a result, neither side always manages to create enough chances to secure a victory.

Several factors can contribute to a draw:

  • Strong defending: Organised defensive lines can limit clear scoring opportunities.
  • Goalkeeper performance: Important saves can prevent a team from taking the lead.
  • Tactical caution: Some teams prioritise avoiding defeat, especially away from home.
  • Missed chances: A team may create opportunities without converting them into goals.
  • Player fatigue: Tired players may struggle to maintain attacking intensity late in a match.
  • Similar team strength: Two evenly matched sides may cancel out each other's strengths.

A goalless draw does not necessarily mean that the match was uninteresting. Both teams might have created chances, defended under pressure and competed for possession. The final score only records the goals, not every important moment during the game.

Common Misunderstandings About Draws

Every drawn match goes to penalties

This is incorrect. Most league matches finish as draws without a penalty shootout. Penalties are used only when the competition format requires a winner and its regulations provide for that procedure.

A draw means both teams played equally well

Not necessarily. A team may dominate possession and create more chances but still finish level. The scoreline records goals, not every measure of performance.

Extra time is always played after a draw

Extra time is not automatic. It depends on the competition rules. In many league fixtures, the referee ends the match after normal time and any applicable added time.

A team loses its points if it draws away from home

In the standard league points system, both teams receive one point regardless of which side is playing at home. The location does not change the basic points awarded for a draw.

A penalty shootout changes the original match score

A shootout determines which team advances or wins a competition tie. It is recorded separately from the score at the end of normal or extra time.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What happens when a football match ends in a draw?

Both teams finish with the same number of goals. In a standard league match, each team usually receives one point. In a knockout competition, the rules may require extra time or a penalty shootout to determine a winner.

2. How many points does a team get for a draw?

Under the common three-points-for-a-win system, each team receives one point for a draw. The exact competition regulations should always be checked.

3. Does every football draw go to extra time?

No. Most league matches simply finish as draws. Extra time is generally used when a competition requires a winner and its rules provide for it.

4. What happens if a knockout match is still level after extra time?

The competition may use a penalty shootout to decide which team advances. The specific procedure is set out in the tournament regulations.

5. Can a football match finish 0–0?

Yes. If neither team scores during the match, the final result is a goalless draw.

6. What happens if both teams draw in a two-legged tie?

The aggregate score is considered. If it remains level, the competition's rules determine the next step, which may include extra time and penalties.

7. Does a draw affect goal difference?

Yes. Goal difference is calculated from goals scored minus goals conceded. A 1–1 draw changes neither team's goal difference, while a 2–2 draw also leaves the difference unchanged.

8. Can a team qualify after drawing a match?

Yes. In a league or group format, a team may qualify based on its overall points and other ranking criteria. In a knockout tie, it may advance through the applicable extra-time or penalty procedure.

9. Is a penalty shootout part of normal playing time?

No. Penalty shootouts take place after the match has ended. They are a separate method of deciding a winner when required by competition rules.

10. Who decides whether extra time or penalties are used?

The competition organiser establishes the relevant regulations. The referee applies the Laws of the Game and follows those regulations during the match.

Final Thoughts

When a football match ends in a draw, the next step depends on the type of competition. In most league fixtures, both teams receive one point and the result is added to the standings. There is no automatic requirement to play extra time or take penalties.

Knockout competitions are different because they often need one team to advance. Their regulations may provide for extra time and, if the score remains level, a penalty shootout. Two-legged ties also require supporters to consider the aggregate score rather than looking at only one match.

Understanding these rules makes football results easier to follow. Whether a match finishes 0–0, 1–1 or 2–2, a draw is a legitimate outcome. Its impact on a team's season depends on the competition, the standings and what the club is trying to achieve.
